## Production policy

Rule (D85): in the realistic scenario (fatigue + new donors), among the learning policies with or without the cooldown, the most donations wins; alerts per day are fixed, so that is also the fewest alerts per donation. Thompson sampling, the incumbent, is kept unless the winner beats its best variant by more than the winner's seed-to-seed std.

Best: **epsilon_greedy+cooldown7**; best Thompson variant: **thompson+cooldown7**; margin 5.1 donations. Chosen: **thompson+cooldown7**.

Regret = donations the oracle got minus donations the policy got, cumulative to that day (lower is better). The oracle ranks by true rate every day; with eligibility gaps that is a strong reference, not a proven optimum, so slightly negative regret is possible.

Hidden response rates are drawn from Beta(1.5, 5.0) (mean 23%, anchored to the real UCI donors' 23.8%) by `simulate_donors()`, blood types by population share. Fatigue: each earlier alert multiplies the reply rate by 0.7, its effect halving every 14 days; new donors: 100/year. Both are assumptions (no study of alert frequency vs response was found). Time of day, distance and donors leaving the registry aren't modelled.
